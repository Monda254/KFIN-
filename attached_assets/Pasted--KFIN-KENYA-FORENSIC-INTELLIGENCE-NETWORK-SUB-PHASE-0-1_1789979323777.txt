# 1. MISSION

You are implementing **KFIN Sub-Phase 0.1**.

Your task is to create the formal **KFIN Development Constitution** and the supporting governance artifacts that will control how KFIN is developed throughout all subsequent implementation phases.

This is not a generic software-development README.

KFIN is being designed as a national forensic intelligence and information-management platform involving potentially sensitive, legally significant and operationally important information.

The development constitution must therefore establish engineering rules appropriate for a system that must eventually support:

* forensic evidence;
* DNA information;
* forensic profiles;
* case information;
* persons;
* missing persons;
* unidentified remains;
* offender information;
* elimination databases;
* chain of custody;
* provenance;
* auditability;
* historical reconstruction;
* national interoperability;
* intelligence analysis;
* graph relationships;
* institutional access;
* clearance-based access;
* privacy;
* data governance;
* cybersecurity;
* resilience;
* controlled analytical capabilities;
* carefully governed AI.

**Do not implement those operational domains in this sub-phase.**

Instead, establish the rules that will ensure they are implemented correctly later.

# 2. FUNDAMENTAL OBJECTIVE

At the completion of 0.1, KFIN must have a written and version-controlled **development constitution** that answers:

> How must every future person, team, service, database, API, AI agent, infrastructure component and implementation phase behave while building KFIN?

The constitution must prevent future development from becoming:

* ad hoc;
* undocumented;
* architecturally inconsistent;
* insecure;
* difficult to audit;

* overly dependent on individual developers;

# 11. SECTION 3 — KFIN ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES

Define the fundamental engineering principles.

At minimum include:

## 11.1 Security by Design

Security must be considered before implementation rather than added after functionality exists.

## 11.2 Privacy by Design

Systems must minimize unnecessary exposure and collection of sensitive information.

## 11.3 Least Privilege

Every user, service and process should receive only the access required.

## 11.4 Explicit Architecture

Important architectural decisions must be visible and documented.

## 11.5 Traceability

Requirements must be traceable to implementation and verification.

## 11.6 Reproducibility

Development and deployment processes should be repeatable.

## 11.7 Testability

Components should be designed so their behavior can be verified.

## 11.8 Fail Securely

Failure must not silently weaken security.

## 11.9 No Silent Data Mutation

Sensitive information must not be modified without controlled mechanisms.

## 11.10 No False Success

The system and development process must never report success when verification has not occurred.

## 11.11 Minimal Trust

Trust must be explicit rather than assumed.

## 11.12 Separation of Duties

Where appropriate, critical operations should require separation of responsibilities.

## 11.13 Evidence Preservation

Future forensic information must be capable of preserving integrity and provenance.

## 11.14 Controlled Change

Important changes must be deliberate, reviewable and traceable.

# 72. FORENSIC-SPECIFIC DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

The constitution must include a dedicated subsection recognizing that future KFIN development differs from ordinary commercial CRUD applications.

Future implementation must assume that certain information may be:

* legally significant;
* operationally significant;
* sensitive;
* subject to retention requirements;
* subject to disclosure controls;
* subject to audit;
* subject to historical reconstruction.

Therefore:

**Correctness is not merely whether the current UI displays the expected value.**

Correctness may also involve:

* who changed it;
* when;
* why;
* under which authorization;
* what existed before;
* what exists now;
* what evidence supports the change.

---

# 73. CHAIN-OF-CUSTODY DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLE

The constitution must recognize chain-of-custody requirements as a future domain concern.

Development must not introduce patterns that make it impossible to determine:

```text
WHO
 ↓
POSSESSED
 ↓
WHAT
 ↓
WHEN
 ↓
FROM WHERE
 ↓
TO WHERE
 ↓
UNDER WHAT AUTHORIZATION
 ↓
WITH WHAT RESULT
```

The detailed workflow belongs to later forensic workflow phases.

---

# 74. PROVENANCE PRINCIPLE

Future KFIN information should have sufficient provenance to establish where information originated and how it was transformed.

Development must avoid uncontrolled transformations that destroy provenance.

---

# 75. TEMPORAL DATA PRINCIPLE

The constitution must recognize that KFIN will eventually require distinction between:

* current state;
* historical state;
* event time;
* system time;
* effective time;
* recorded time.

The exact implementation belongs to the approved data architecture.

---

# 76. DATA LINEAGE PRINCIPLE

Future analytical outputs must be capable of being traced back to their source information.

This is particularly important for:

* intelligence;
* graph analysis;
* reporting;
* analytical decision support;
* future AI-assisted capabilities.

---

# 77. AI AND FORENSIC DECISION PRINCIPLE

The constitution must explicitly prevent uncontrolled AI automation in sensitive forensic decision-making.

AI-generated output must not automatically become:

* forensic fact;
* legal conclusion;
* investigative conclusion;
* DNA interpretation;
* evidence determination.

Future AI features must have explicit governance, explainability and human oversight appropriate to their use.

---

# 78. INTEROPERABILITY PRINCIPLE

Future KFIN integrations must use controlled contracts.

No institution should gain unrestricted database-level access merely because systems need to exchange information.

Future exchange should consider:

* identity;
* authorization;
* purpose;
* data classification;
* schema;
* provenance;
* validation;
* audit;
* rate limits;
* failure;
* replay;
* versioning.

---

# 79. NATIONAL-SCALE PRINCIPLE

The constitution must acknowledge that KFIN is intended to evolve toward a national-scale ecosystem.

Therefore development should avoid assumptions that:

* only one institution will use the system;
* only one user type exists;
* all users have the same clearance;
* all data has the same classification;
* all integrations are trusted equally;
* all workloads are small.

---

# 80. MULTI-INSTITUTION PRINCIPLE

Future architecture must recognize institutional boundaries.

A user may belong to:

* a forensic laboratory;
* law enforcement;
* another authorized government institution;
* a participating partner organization;
* an administrative authority.

The exact institutions and authorization policies must come from the approved KFIN specifications.

Do not invent institutional permissions.

---

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓
